December 2026 Tax Due Dates: Advance Tax, GSTR-9 and Belated ITR – A Simple Checklist

Three big deadlines fall in December 2026: the advance tax instalment on 15 December, and the GSTR-9 annual return and belated income tax return on 31 December. Here is who is covered and what to prepare now.

December is a heavy month for tax compliance. Apart from the usual monthly GST and TDS dates, three year-end deadlines fall in December 2026. If you start preparing in November, none of them needs to become a last-day rush. Here is a plain-language guide for small businesses, professionals and salaried people in Delhi.

1. Advance tax – 15 December 2026. The third instalment of advance tax for FY 2026-27 is due on 15 December. By this date, 75% of your estimated tax for the year should have been paid (including the June and September instalments). Advance tax applies when your estimated tax for the year, after reducing TDS and TCS, is ₹10,000 or more. This is not only for businesses – a salaried person with capital gains, rent, interest or freelance income can also be covered.

Who gets relief. Resident senior citizens (60 years or above) who have no business or professional income do not have to pay advance tax. Taxpayers on the presumptive scheme for small businesses and professionals can pay the whole amount in one instalment by 15 March 2027. For everyone else, a shortfall in an instalment generally attracts interest at 1% per month, so it is cheaper to estimate properly now.

2. GSTR-9 annual return – 31 December 2026. The GST annual return for FY 2025-26 is due on 31 December 2026, and the forms are already open on the GST portal. GSTR-9 is compulsory for regular taxpayers with aggregate turnover above ₹2 crore; below that, filing is optional under Notification No. 15/2025-Central Tax. Businesses with turnover above ₹5 crore also have to file the self-certified reconciliation statement in GSTR-9C by the same date.

Why it needs time. GSTR-9 is a summary of the whole year, so your sales, tax paid and input tax credit should match across GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B and your books before you file. Differences found now can be studied calmly; differences found on 30 December usually cannot.

3. Belated income tax return – 31 December 2026. If you missed the original due date for filing your ITR for AY 2026-27 (income earned in FY 2025-26), you can still file a belated return up to 31 December 2026. A late fee applies: ₹1,000 if your total income is up to ₹5 lakh and ₹5,000 if it is higher, plus interest on any unpaid tax. Filing late also means business and capital losses of the year generally cannot be carried forward, although house property loss can. After 31 December, the only route left is an updated return, which carries additional tax.

Do not forget the monthly dates. The regular December dates continue alongside: TDS/TCS deposit for November by 7 December, GSTR-1 by 11 December and GSTR-3B by 20 December for monthly filers.

What you should do now:

  • Estimate your full-year income, including capital gains, rent and interest, and work out the advance tax payable by 15 December
  • Check Form 26AS and AIS so that TDS already deducted is counted correctly
  • Check your FY 2025-26 turnover to see whether GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C apply to you
  • Reconcile GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B with your books for the full year
  • If your ITR for AY 2026-27 is still pending, collect Form 16, bank statements and capital gains statements and file well before 31 December
  • Keep a note of the 7, 11 and 20 December monthly dates so routine filings do not slip

This article is a general summary for awareness. Due dates can be extended and conditions depend on your facts and on the exact wording of the law and notifications, so please confirm the position before acting.
